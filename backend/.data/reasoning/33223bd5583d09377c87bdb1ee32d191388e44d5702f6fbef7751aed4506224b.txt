## 4. CONTRACT EXECUTION

### Gainshare Model Performance

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Revenue Model** | 30% of verified fuel savings (gainshare) **or** flat subscription |
| **Upfront Cost to Customer** | **$0** — zero capital outlay |
| **Fuel Savings (Verified Basis)** | 8% (financial model), 7% average (live), 15% max (live human-operated), 25% max (simulation) |
| **Deployment Method** | Tablet-based, software-first. No hardware installation. Locomotive-agnostic |
| **PTC Requirement** | None — advisory mode operates independently |
| **Pilot Duration** | 60–90 day corridor pilot to prove fuel savings |
| **Deployment Timeline** | 90 days from contract to live deployment |

### Contract Structure
- **Pilot → Production conversion**: 60–90 day validation period, then convert to multi-year gainshare agreement
- **Average deal size**: Not yet established (pre-revenue). Projected per-property ARR varies by fuel spend and fleet size. G&W near-term = ~$24K/property/year ($2.7M ÷ 112 properties)
- **Renewal rates**: N/A — no contracts have yet reached renewal stage
- **Billing basis**: Verified fuel savings, measured and documented per corridor

### What Makes This Model Work Commercially
1. **Zero customer risk** — they pay nothing unless they save money
2. **Self-proving** — the 90-day pilot IS the sales process AND the validation
3. **Aligned incentives** — we make more when they save more
4. **Low switching cost for entry** — but high switching cost once operational data is embedded

### What Concerns Me
- Gainshare variability: Fuel savings fluctuate with terrain, crew compliance, weather, and commodity mix. We need a **floor mechanism** or hybrid (gainshare + minimum subscription) to stabilize revenue predictability.
- Verification disputes: Who audits the fuel savings? We need a third-party or algorithmic verification standard before we scale.
